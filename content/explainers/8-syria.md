# Syria

**Assume zero prior knowledge. A case study in how one civil war pulls in every outside power.**

Syria sits at the crossroads of the Middle East. For decades it was ruled by the Assad family, a dictatorship.

**The war:** in 2011, peaceful protests (part of the "Arab Spring" uprisings) were crushed, and Syria spiralled into a brutal civil war. It fractured into zones held by the government, rebel groups, Kurdish forces, and jihadists (ISIS controlled a large area for years). Outside powers poured in:
- **Russia and Iran** propped up Assad.
- **The US** backed Kurdish-led forces against ISIS.
- **Turkey** intervened against Kurdish groups.
- **Israel** struck Iranian targets inside Syria.

It became the textbook case of a local war turned into an **everyone's proxy battlefield** (see [[3-proxy-warfare]]).

**The turn:** in late 2024, a sudden rebel offensive toppled the Assad government — a huge shock that reshuffled the whole board and dealt a blow to both Russia and Iran, who lost a key ally.

**Why it connects:**
- **Refugees** — over a decade of displacement reshaped Lebanon, Turkey, Jordan and European politics (the [[6-displacement-and-refugees]] thread).
- **The Iran axis** — Syria was the land bridge linking Iran to Hezbollah in Lebanon.

**The lens:** Syria shows how [[4-russia]], [[1-iran]], and the US all use one country as a chessboard.
