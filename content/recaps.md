# How we got here — the past year, per region

*A quick "if you've been away" catch-up. Edit `content/recaps.md` to keep it current.*

## Iran War
Started **Feb 28, 2026** with US/Israeli strikes that killed Iran's Supreme Leader. Five weeks of missiles, drones, and a closed Strait of Hormuz followed, then an April ceasefire that never really held. Iran named the killed leader's son as successor. By July the ceasefire was declared "over" and stop-start US strikes resumed. The through-line: **oil and shipping keep this a global economic story, not just a regional one.**

## Gaza / Israel
A US-brokered ceasefire has technically held since **October 2025**, but strikes continued and the agreed "Yellow Line" for Israeli withdrawal has reportedly shifted on the ground. Israeli domestic politics moved rightward under the prolonged war; Hamas elected a new, weaker leader. Casualty totals differ sharply by source — compare them directly rather than trusting one.

## Western Sahara
The diplomatic momentum kept **tilting toward Rabat**: France plus more African, Arab, and Latin American states endorsed Morocco's autonomy plan, and US lawmakers visited Dakhla. The counter-current is legal — an EU court ruling on Sahrawi consultation that opponents keep citing. Algeria and Polisario still demand a referendum.

## Sudan
The **SAF vs RSF** war (since April 2023) ground on as the world's largest displacement crisis, now defined by heavy drone use on both sides — described as a preview of future African warfare. Still badly under-covered globally.

## The Sahel (Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger)
The post-coup governments completed their pivot from **France to Russia** (Wagner / "Africa Corps"). Security did not improve — deadly ambushes continued — but the alliance map of the region changed for years to come.
