# Timeline — Syria

*Year by year. Bold = the year.*

**1971** — Hafez al-Assad takes power, founding a family dictatorship that will last over 50 years.

**2000** — His son **Bashar al-Assad** inherits the presidency.

**2011** — Arab Spring protests erupt; the government's violent crackdown triggers civil war.

**2013** — A chemical-weapons attack near Damascus crosses a Western "red line" but brings no major intervention. The war deepens.

**2014** — **ISIS** seizes a vast area of Syria and Iraq, declaring a "caliphate." A US-led coalition and Kurdish forces begin fighting it.

**2015** — **Russia** intervenes militarily to save Assad, alongside **Iran** and Hezbollah. The war becomes a full proxy battlefield.

**2016–2018** — Assad, with Russian and Iranian help, retakes major cities like Aleppo. ISIS's territory is gradually destroyed.

**2019–2023** — The war freezes into zones of control; millions of refugees remain abroad; reconstruction stalls under sanctions.

**2024** — **December:** a sudden rebel offensive topples Assad — a stunning collapse that ends 50+ years of family rule and weakens both Russia and Iran, who lose a key ally.

**2025–2026** — A fragile transition: rival factions, outside powers, and Syria's neighbours jockey over what comes next, with the refugee question still central to the region.
